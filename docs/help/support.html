When something goes wrong

Look through the usual suspects below. If none of them is it, write to me and I will try to work out what happened.

I cannot find my tasks

First, clear the search, the category and project filters, and the flag. Then look in the other stacks and in the Timeline; a task you ticked is there. If a second device shows something different, read the sync checklist before you replace or restore anything.

If you moved from a Mac version you built yourself to the TestFlight one, the app keeps its data in a different place, so it starts empty. Nothing is lost. If you used an iCloud sync file, choose Use Existing Sync File… and point it at the same file, with Merge.

A reminder stayed silent

  • Are notifications allowed for DayHand in the system settings? Is a Focus mode holding them back?
  • Reminders go off at 9 a.m. on their day. A reminder for today, set after 9, has no notification coming.
  • A completed task, or a reminder you cleared, will not notify. One you already answered for that day has no reason to.
  • Open DayHand. It refreshes its schedule when it starts, which matters most after you have changed something on another device.

My imported tasks look different

Each importer has its limits with dates, repeating tasks and extra details. The import guide lists them. Look in the task’s Notes first: what DayHand could not place is usually kept there as text.

Tell me about it

DayHand is made by one person, so there is no ticket desk. During the beta, use Send Beta Feedback in TestFlight. You can also open an issue on GitHub, for a bug, a question or an idea. That needs a GitHub account; reading these pages does not.

It helps a lot if you tell me:

  • your device, its system version, and the DayHand version and build;
  • what you did, what you expected, and what happened;
  • the exact message on the screen, if there was one;
  • for sync trouble, which devices, and what the sync status says on each.
GitHub issues are public. Blank out private task titles in screenshots. Do not attach your task file or a backup, and no passwords or personal details. A small invented example that shows the same problem is better anyway.

Before you write, you can browse the existing reports; someone may have met it already.
