Help and support

DayHand Help

A short manual for a small app. New here? Start at the top. Something broken? Go to Troubleshooting, and if that does not help, write to me.

Your first tasks

Start with a handful of real tasks. Projects and categories can wait until you feel the need for them.

1. Capture something

Tap +, type the task, save it. It lands in Inbox, which is where things wait until you have decided what to do with them. A task needs nothing else: no project, no date.

2. Give it a place

Tap a task (click, on a Mac) to see what you can do with it. Moving it is the main thing. Put what you are working on in Now. It can stay there for days or weeks; nothing pushes it out. Next is what you will pick up after that, Later is for what you want to keep but are not touching, and Waiting is for what is stuck on somebody else.

The next section explains the stacks properly.

3. Keep related things together

Choose Edit in a task’s actions to change its details and add notes.

A project gathers the tasks of one piece of work: a trip, a course, a client. A category is a broad part of your life, such as Work or Home. A group collects projects. A project belongs to one category and can sit in one group, and changing either changes it for every task in the project. A task without a project can still have a category.

4. Find your way around

Search and the filter help you focus on one project or category. On a Mac, and on an iPad held wide, a sidebar lists your categories, groups and projects. From the filter you can rename and rearrange them.

Under More → Appearance you can choose Automatic, Light or Dark. Automatic follows your device.

5. Finish

Tick the circle when the work is done. A finished task leaves the stacks but is not gone: More → Timeline lists everything you have finished, by day, and you can reopen a task you ticked too soon.

When the lists start to feel stale, try a review.

Try this. Add “Plan the garden”. Move it to Now while you collect ideas. When you ask a contractor for a quote, move it to Waiting and set a reminder for the day you want to chase them. Same task from start to finish.

Where a task lives, and when to look at it

A stack says what you intend to do with a task. A date says when you want to be reminded of it. DayHand keeps the two apart on purpose.

The five stacks

Inbox
What you have just captured and not yet placed.
Now
What you are actively working on, even if it takes several days.
Next
What you will pick up when you have room. It does not mean tomorrow.
Later
Ideas and plans you want to keep, and are not touching yet.
Waiting
Work that needs a reply, a delivery, a decision or someone else’s part before you can go on. Different from Later: here you would carry on if you could.

A date never moves a task. Time passing changes nothing about where it sits; you decide when it moves.

Here is one task over a week. On Monday you add “Prepare the presentation”, with a reminder for Tuesday and a deadline of Friday. Tuesday the reminder tells you to start, so you move it to Now. Something more urgent turns up, so it goes to Later with a new reminder for Wednesday. Wednesday it returns to Now. Thursday you send a draft to a colleague and park it in Waiting. Friday the feedback arrives, you finish it, and you tick it off. One task, one deadline that never changed, and nothing was ever “overdue”.

Reminders: look at this again

Pick Remind me… from a task’s actions, or set one in the editor. Change reminder… picks another day, and Clear the reminder removes it.

A reminder goes off at 9 a.m. on the day you chose, provided notifications are allowed for DayHand. If you set one for today after 9, there is nothing left to fire: the task is marked as due now and no notification follows. The date picker says so when that happens. (Reminders made by older versions may keep the time they were given.)

Tap a notification and DayHand opens that task. Swiping it away is not an answer; the reminder is still waiting for you in the app. You answer it by Keep here (it is in the right stack, thanks), by moving the task, by completing it, or by choosing another day.

Colours, the flag and the badge

  • Orange edge: the reminder is coming within the next three days.
  • Red edge: the reminder is today, or already past. It means “you wanted to look at this”, not “you are late”.
  • The flag in the app: gathers every task with a red or orange edge, from any stack. The number on it counts the red ones.
  • The badge on the app icon: counts reminders that have arrived and that you have not answered yet.

So the flag and the badge can disagree, and that is fine. Answer a reminder with Keep here and the badge goes down, while the task keeps its red edge and stays in the flag until you change or clear the date.

Deadlines: finished by then

Add a deadline in the editor when there is a real finish line: a submission, a renewal, a delivery. To add a new one you set a reminder first, and the deadline field appears after it. If you later clear the reminder, a deadline you already set stays visible and editable.

A deadline sends no notification and moves nothing. It is simply written on the task. Set the reminder early enough to do something about it.

No notification? See what to check.

Reviews

Open More → Review and pick a helper. Each one asks a single question about a single task or project, so you decide instead of scanning a long list.

Reviewing tasks

Review Inbox
Where does this belong? Move new tasks to the stack that fits, or leave them if you are not ready.
Review Now
Am I still working on this? Keep what is active, move what has changed, complete what is done.
Review Waiting
Can this move forward? Keep what is really blocked, bring back what is not, or set a reminder to chase.
Review Next and Later
Is this still relevant? Bring forward what matters and let go of what does not.
Review all tasks
Inbox, Now, Waiting, then Next and Later, in one pass. A task you move during the pass does not come back later in the same pass.

The helper shows the task, and how long it has been in its stack. Keep it where it is, move it, edit it, complete it or delete it. The stack buttons stay in the same place from one task to the next, so your thumb learns them.

You can stop in the middle and come back. Your place is saved on that device only; another device starts its own pass. Tasks that arrive in the meantime wait for the next pass.

A review is a decision, not a deadline. “Keep in Now” is a perfectly good answer, even for a task that has been there a month.

Reviewing projects

Review projects asks one question for each project: does it have a clear way forward? You see its tasks by stack, with its category and group.

  • Looks right — next project: fine, move on.
  • Add a task: write down the missing step, with this project already selected.
  • Review these tasks: go through this project’s tasks one at a time.
  • Edit project: change its name, category or group.

After adding, editing or reviewing you come back to the same project. The chevron at the top left goes back to the project you just left, and the cross leaves the review.

Projects with unfinished work come first. Projects with nothing left to do come after, so you can archive them. Archived projects are skipped, and tasks without a project show up in the task reviews instead.

Archiving a project that still has open tasks asks you to complete them first. Archiving is for finished work, not a way to put a project to sleep; for that, use Later.

Looking back

More → Timeline, and the History of a single project, show what you have finished. They are separate from the helpers, which are about the work still open.

Sync

DayHand shares your tasks through one file in your own iCloud Drive. There is no DayHand account and no server. On a single device you do not need any of this.

The first device

  1. Make sure iCloud Drive is on.
  2. Open More → Sync → Create Sync File….
  3. Save the file in an iCloud Drive folder you will find again.
  4. Give iCloud a moment to upload it before you set up the next device.

Every other device

  1. Sign in with the same Apple Account, and make sure the file has downloaded.
  2. Open More → Sync → Use Existing Sync File….
  3. Choose Merge With This Device, which keeps the tasks on both sides.
  4. Pick the same file you created on the first device. Do not create a second file with the same name; two files with one name are two different files.
  5. Look at your tasks and at the sync status on both devices.
Replace is a different thing. “Replace Tasks on This Device” throws away what is on this device and takes the file’s tasks instead. If you might want the current list back, make a backup first.

DayHand checks that the file you pick really is a DayHand file. A spreadsheet, somebody else’s JSON or a photo is refused, because syncing writes into the file and would overwrite it.

Day to day

Each device keeps its own copy, so you can work offline. Changes meet through the file once iCloud has delivered it and DayHand has read it. That can take a while, and an app that is closed does not hurry. Do not expect it to be instant.

Avoid editing the same task on two devices while both are offline. If two versions of one task conflict, the most recent edit wins. DayHand does not stitch two edits of the same title together.

When the devices disagree

  1. Look at More → Sync on each device. “Not syncing” means no file is linked there.
  2. Check that both use the same file in the same place. The same name is not enough.
  3. Check that iCloud Drive is connected and that the file has finished downloading, in Files or in Finder.
  4. Open DayHand on both devices and tap Sync Now. Read the line under the button.
  5. If you moved or renamed the file and a device lost it, back up that device, stop using the old link, and pick the right file again, choosing Merge.

Disconnecting

Stop Using This File disconnects this device. Its tasks stay. If you only want to look at a saved copy once, without linking anything, use Restore From File… instead.

Still stuck? Tell me, and say what the sync status shows on each device.

Imports, exports and backups

Your tasks are yours: get them in, take them out, and get them back. An import is a one-time copy. DayHand does not stay connected to the app it came from.

From Apple Reminders

  1. Open More → Tasks → Import from Apple Reminders….
  2. Allow access to Reminders. Apple only offers “full access” for reading lists. DayHand reads and never changes them.
  3. Pick the lists, the stack the tasks should go to, a project or category, and whether to bring the due dates.
  4. Look at the preview, then confirm.

A due date becomes a DayHand reminder at 9 a.m., not a deadline, and the original date and time are kept in the Notes. Repeating reminders are skipped. Subtasks, tags, attachments and smart-list rules are not carried over. Notes, links, locations and priorities come along as text where they exist.

A backup is taken first. Import the same lists again on the same device and the tasks you already imported are skipped, including the ones you deleted. On another device, or after resetting your account, you may get duplicates.

From Todoist

  1. In Todoist, export your projects as CSV files.
  2. Open More → Tasks → Import from Todoist… and pick one or more of those files.
  3. For each file, choose the stack, project and category, and look at the date options.
  4. Read the preview and any warnings, then import.

Todoist’s project export does not include completed tasks. Dates it recognises become reminders at 9 a.m.; deadlines stay deadlines. Descriptions, comments and the other details it can read go into the Notes. Subtasks become tasks of their own, with their parent and section noted. Attachments are not downloaded. Repeating schedules are not recreated. A date DayHand does not understand is flagged for you; if you import the task anyway, the original text of the date is kept in the Notes.

A backup is taken first. Importing an unchanged file under the same name skips the rows already imported. Edit or rename the export and you may get duplicates.

Mind your alerts. Apple Reminders and Todoist keep sending their own notifications, and a future reminder you import will also notify you from DayHand. Decide which app gets to nag you.

DayHand CSV

More → Tasks → Export as CSV… saves a portable copy. Import from CSV… offers Add to Existing Tasks, which merges a DayHand CSV into your work, or Replace All Tasks, which clears the current tasks first. Think twice before choosing the second.

For Todoist files, use the Todoist importer. CSV files from other apps will not match DayHand’s format. If you want to prepare a file yourself, export a few tasks first and copy the layout.

Backups

Under More → Backups, tap Back Up Now before anything big. DayHand also makes its own, on days you open the app, in a few rotating slots: yesterday, the day before, and about a week ago. They are a safety net, not an archive. A second manual backup on the same day replaces the first.

To keep a copy somewhere of your own choosing, use a backup’s Share action. On a Mac, right-click a backup for Restore, Share and Delete. On an iPhone or iPad, press and hold. Swiping works too.

Restoring

  1. If you might want the current tasks back, back them up or export them first.
  2. Tap a backup in the list, or choose Restore From File… and pick any DayHand file.
  3. Read the two counts in the confirmation: the tasks here, and the tasks in the file. Restoring replaces the current tasks, and brings back the categories and projects too.
  4. Confirm only if this is the copy you want.

Restore From File only reads the file. It does not link it for syncing. If you are syncing, that stays as it was, so what you restore will travel to your other devices. To look at a copy on its own, stop syncing first.

For a continuing link to a shared file, see Sync.

Free and paid

The plan is a free app with one purchase to unlock the rest. No subscription.

Where things stand. This is a beta, and everything is unlocked. The purchase, the 30-day trial and Restore Purchases are not built yet. What follows is the plan, so you know what to expect. A testing switch in the beta lets me try the limits on my own devices.

Free

Unlimited tasks, categories and groups, up to three active projects, and the last 30 days of finished tasks. The reviews, notes, reminders, deadlines, sync, imports, exports and backups are all included. I do not want the useful parts behind a wall.

The unlock

One purchase, once: unlimited active projects and your full history of finished tasks, on your iPhone, iPad and Mac, with the same Apple Account. I will put the price here when it exists.

At the limit

Archived projects do not count, so archiving a finished project frees a place, and unarchiving one takes a place. Capturing a task is never blocked. A project you already have stays readable and editable, even if an import brings you over three.

Finished tasks older than 30 days are only hidden from the Timeline. Search still finds them, and they stay in your backups and your CSV exports. Nothing is ever deleted because you did not pay.

The trial

I plan a 30-day trial that you start yourself. When it ends, the app goes back to the free version. No charge, and nothing happens automatically. It is not available yet, and neither is Restore Purchases. I will explain both here when they ship.

Troubleshooting

Look through the usual suspects below. If none of them is it, write to me and I will try to work out what happened.

I cannot find my tasks

First, clear the search, the category and project filters, and the flag. Then look in the other stacks and in the Timeline; a task you ticked is there. If a second device shows something different, read the sync checklist before you replace or restore anything.

A reminder stayed silent

  • Are notifications allowed for DayHand in the system settings? Is a Focus mode holding them back?
  • Reminders go off at 9 a.m. on their day. A reminder for today, set after 9, has no notification coming.
  • A completed task, or a reminder you cleared, will not notify. One you already answered for that day has no reason to.
  • Open DayHand. It refreshes its schedule when it starts, which matters most after you have changed something on another device.

If the flag and the badge on the icon show different numbers, that is on purpose. Here is why.

My imported tasks look different

Each importer has its limits with dates, repeating tasks and extra details. The import guide lists them. Look in the task’s Notes first: what DayHand could not place is usually kept there as text.

Contact

DayHand is made by one person, so there is no ticket desk. The best way to reach me is to open an issue on GitHub, for a bug, a question or an idea. That needs a GitHub account; reading this page does not.

It helps a lot if you tell me:

  • your device, its system version, and the DayHand version and build;
  • what you did, what you expected, and what happened;
  • the exact message on the screen, if there was one;
  • for sync trouble, which devices, and what the sync status says on each.
GitHub issues are public. Blank out private task titles in screenshots. Do not attach your task file or a backup, and no passwords or personal details. A small invented example that shows the same problem is better anyway.

Before you write, you can browse the existing reports; someone may have met it already.
