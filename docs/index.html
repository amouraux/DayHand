DayHand

Organise your tasks around your life and your objectives, not the calendar.

Preparing a talk, planning a trip, building a feature, renovating a room — much of what matters cannot fit neatly into a daily checklist. Yet many task managers encourage you to assign a date before you know when the work will happen. When that day passes, unfinished tasks become overdue. You reschedule them and feel behind, even when the work is progressing as it should.

DayHand lets a task stay active for as long as it takes, without having to assign it a new day each morning. Organise your life and work around what you are doing Now, what comes Next, and what belongs Later. Capture new tasks in Inbox until you are ready to decide.

If you already use a task manager, DayHand will feel familiar: tasks, projects, notes and reminders. What feels refreshing is organising work around your intentions, with room for tasks to take several days and guided reviews to help you reconsider what matters.

A place for every task

Inspired by Kanban, DayHand adapts the simplicity of cards and stacks to managing your life and work. A stack reflects where a task belongs in your life right now, rather than a day on the calendar.

Inbox

Capture a task before deciding what to do with it. New tasks normally land here, ready to sort when you have a moment. If you already know where a task belongs, choose a different stack as you add it.

Now

What you’re actively working on. A task can stay here for several days or weeks while you move it forward. Now means in progress; it is not a promise to finish everything today.

Next

What you intend to pick up next, once you have room to take it on. Keep your next steps in view without assigning them to tomorrow or making them compete with the work already in progress.

Later

Ideas, plans and tasks you want to keep for the future. You’re choosing not to take them on yet, but they have a place to live until you are ready.

Waiting

Work you cannot move forward until a reply, a delivery, someone’s feedback, or something else arrives. Keep it out of your active work, then decide where it belongs when you can act again.

You decide when a task moves. Time passing changes nothing about its stack. Tick it off when the work is finished, and it goes to Completed.

Dates with a purpose

Most tasks do not need a date. When a date matters, distinguish when you want to think about the task from when it really must be finished.

Reminders: when to think about it

Choose a day to bring a task back to your attention — to start work, reconsider an idea, or follow up on something in Waiting. A notification and a coloured edge help you notice it. Change or clear the reminder when your plans change; the task stays where you put it.

Deadlines: when it must be finished

Add an optional deadline alongside a reminder for a real commitment: a submission, a renewal, a delivery. It is shown on the task as a finish date. The reminder handles notifications and the coloured edge; the deadline does not send a separate alert or move the task.

Neither date chooses a stack, moves a task or marks it completed. A task can stay in Now all week, with a reminder on Monday and a deadline on Friday.

The journey of a task

You’re asked to prepare a presentation for Friday. It is one task, even when priorities change and the work takes several days.

  1. MondayCapture “Prepare the presentation”, with a reminder to start Tuesday and a deadline of Friday. Inbox
  2. TuesdayThe reminder brings it back to your attention. You decide to start and move it to Now. Now
  3. Later that daySomething more urgent needs your attention. Put the presentation aside and set a reminder for Wednesday. Later
  4. WednesdayThe reminder prompts you to pick it up again. Move it back to Now and continue the same task. Now
  5. ThursdaySend the draft to a colleague. You need their feedback before you can finish; set a reminder to follow up on Friday. Waiting
  6. FridayFollow up when reminded. The feedback arrives, and you move the task to Now for the final changes. Now
  7. FinishedDeliver the presentation on Friday and tick off the task. The deadline is met. Completed

One task throughout. Its Friday deadline never changed. Reminders brought it back to your attention; you chose when to move it. The pauses and the unticked days were part of getting the work done.

Keep related work together

Projects for a shared purpose

Keep the tasks for a trip, a course, a client or a piece of work together. Start a task with a name such as HOLIDAY and accept the project offered below the field, or choose one from the project button. Each task can have one project, shown as a coloured prefix.

Categories for the parts of your life

Separate broad areas such as Work, Home and Courses with your own labels, icons and colours. A project supplies a starting category for its tasks; you can also give a task a category without assigning it to a project.

Groups for related projects

Gather projects into groups such as Clients, Courses or Holidays. Rename and reorganise them from the Filter. When a project is finished, archive it and keep its tasks available for reference.

Find your focus

Search task titles, project names and group names. Filter by any mix of categories and projects, or pick a whole group. Show projects with open tasks, all active projects, or archived ones when you want to look back.

Guided reviews, when your lists need a rethink

Where does this task belong? Am I still working on this? Can this move forward? Is it still relevant? A review asks one question at a time, so you decide instead of scrolling a long list.

Review tasks

Review a single stack or all your tasks, without repeating tasks you have moved during that pass. Keep a task where it is, move it, edit it or complete it.

Review projects

Walk through your projects to check that each has a clear next step. Add the missing step, review the project’s related tasks, or archive a project that is finished.

See what you’ve accomplished, over time

A list of things to do is only part of the picture. DayHand also keeps a record of the work you finish.

This week, and last week

Review completed tasks grouped by the day you finished them. See what you got done this week, or look back at last week before deciding what comes next.

The longer view

Browse your all-time completion timeline by month and day, or open a project’s History to see its finished work. Daily counts and simple bars make the pattern of completions easy to see.

Completed tasks remain in the Completed stack, so you can find them again or reopen work that needs another pass.

For your life and your work

Architect

Drawings in progress in Now, the next revision in Next, future ideas in Later. A set sent to the client goes to Waiting until feedback arrives. Keep a reminder to follow up and a deadline for the actual submission.

Landscape contractor

A garden taking shape in Now, the next site visit in Next, future planting plans in Later. A job awaiting materials or a client’s approval goes to Waiting. Set a reminder to follow up and a deadline for the agreed handover.

Student

Balance assignments, revision and everyday responsibilities. Keep each course’s tasks in its own project, focus on the work in Now, and use real submission dates as deadlines. Group work awaiting a teammate’s contribution goes to Waiting.

Researcher

Keep analysis and writing in Now while manuscripts with co-authors or experiments awaiting data sit in Waiting. Group related studies and grants, and use project history to look back at what you finished over the course of the work.

Web developer

A feature can stay in Now for several days, with the next one in Next and the backlog in Later. Move work blocked by an API key or client feedback to Waiting. Keep the release deadline without repeatedly rescheduling the task.

Home and family

Sorting out insurance in Now, booking a check-up in Next, holiday ideas in Later. A repair awaiting a tradesperson’s quote goes to Waiting. Set a reminder to follow up and keep the actual renewal date as the deadline.

Your tasks stay yours

No DayHand account or server

DayHand uses your devices and your own iCloud Drive to store and sync your tasks. There is no DayHand account to create and no DayHand server holding your data.

Sync built into the design

A shared file in your own iCloud Drive keeps your tasks in sync across iPhone, iPad and Mac. Choose that same file on each device once, then work on your tasks wherever you are. iCloud Drive connects your devices without a separate DayHand service.

Backups and a way out

Automatic backups are taken on days you open the app, with three rotating copies kept. Take extra backups by hand, restore an earlier copy, or share one somewhere safer. Import from Apple Reminders or a Todoist CSV export, or use DayHand’s own CSV import and export. Imports are copies, with no ongoing sync to the original app.
